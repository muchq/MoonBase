# rummy

The rules engines for Rummy (#245) and Gin (#1610): immutable
`GameState` and `GinState` value types in the style of
[`../castle`](../castle), for the games hub, and `TableState`, the
dealer's-choice table (#1609) that deals one after another.

The rules the engine plays are the contract, stated on `GameState` in
`game_state.h` and pinned one by one in `game_state_test.cc`: seven (or
ten) cards a seat; one card turned up to start the discard pile; a turn is a draw from the stock or the discard — its top, or
down into it, which binds the seat to play the deepest card taken — any
melds and lay-offs, then a discard, and a card taken alone from the
discard may not go straight back unless it is the last in the hand; an empty stock is the discard pile turned over under its
top card; the first seat to empty its hand wins and scores what everyone
else still holds. What makes a meld is `arrangedMeld` in `meld.h`: three
or four of a rank, or three or more of a suit in sequence with the ace low
or high but never both.

```bash
bazel test //domains/games/libs/cards/rummy/...
```

`table_serde.{h,cc}` is the versioned JSON the games hub stores a table
in: the seats, wins and dealer, and the deal in play or just ended, nested
in its own engine's schema — `game_state_serde` for 7-card and 10-card,
`gin_serde` for gin. 7-card is stored as `basic`, its name before the
others. Each is the engine's full truth, stock and every hand included,
so it is server-side only; redaction stays in the hub. The `*_serde_test.cc`
files pin the schemas.

Gin's rules are `GinState`'s, in `gin.h`, pinned in `gin_test.cc`. Its
reckoning needs each hand's best arrangement: `arrange.h`'s search, which
`arrange_test.cc` checks against a brute force and against
`testdata/arrange_corpus.json` — cases generated by an independent Python
oracle (`testdata/make_arrange_corpus.py`). The UI does no arranging of
its own: which cards meld is the player's to see.
